# NRLIS-P001 Minimum Intake Checklist

**Status:** Participant-neutral draft only. Do not ask, answer, retain, or enter
participant-specific responses until WP1 exits and participant-facing intake is
explicitly authorized.

**Privacy rule:** Do not provide name, exact birth date, address, hospital
number, phone number, or other direct identifiers.

Family conversation, ordinary care knowledge, or answers previously mentioned
outside an approved research process must not be silently converted into NRLIS
research data. Once authorized, the completed checklist belongs in approved
private storage rather than Git.

## A. Immediate eligibility and safety

1. Is the communication difficulty long-standing/stable, fluctuating, or
   recently/suddenly worse?
2. Is P001 an adult?
3. Is there a clinician-established diagnosis? If yes, provide only the
   diagnosis label and approximate onset interval.
4. Can P001 currently signal stop or discomfort in any reliable way?

## B. Understanding and authorship

5. Which are understood reliably: familiar spoken dialect, standard Mandarin,
   written Chinese, pictures, gestures, or short demonstrations?
6. Can P001 answer known yes/no questions consistently?
7. Can P001 reject an intentionally wrong option?
8. Who can independently confirm that P001, rather than a caregiver, made the
   selection?

## C. Available access channels

9. Which movements are reliable: finger touch, hand, head, eyes, blink, facial
   gesture, vocal yes/no, or another movement?
10. Can P001 look at and deliberately hold gaze on one of several screen
    targets?
11. What usually causes fatigue or loss of control, and after approximately how
    long?
12. What device is currently available: phone, iPad/tablet, computer, external
    camera, switch, or eye tracker?

## D. Residual speech

13. What can familiar family members understand now: common needs, single
    words, short phrases, or almost nothing?
14. What can unfamiliar listeners understand?
15. Are errors consistent or highly variable?
16. Are repetition, long pauses, initiation difficulty, low volume, or slurring
    observed?
17. Does a prompted word come out more reliably than spontaneous conversation?

Do not infer dysarthria, apraxia, or aphasia from these answers alone.

## E. Language and dialect

18. What is the dialect or regional variety?
19. Can P001 read standard written Chinese?
20. Should the screen display dialect writing, standard written Chinese,
    pictures, or a combination?
21. Should final audio be in dialect, Mandarin, or depend on the setting?

## F. Current communication and first target

22. How does P001 communicate successfully today?
23. Which five to ten low-risk messages would provide the most immediate value?
24. Which messages or topics must never be predicted by AI?
25. What would count as a useful improvement: fewer repetitions, faster
    communication, less caregiver guessing, more privacy, or something else?

## G. Historical voice assets

26. Approximately how much pre-impairment audio exists?
27. Are there clean, single-speaker clips?
28. Are accurate transcripts available, and who understands the dialect well
    enough to create them?
29. Has P001 expressed a preference about hearing a synthetic version of their
    earlier voice?

## H. Research permission path

30. Is the goal currently private family assistance, publication-quality
    research, product development, or a combination?
31. Is there an institution, faculty mentor, clinician, or SLP through whom an
    ethics/IRB determination can be requested?
32. Who other than the family investigator can review consent accessibility and
    ambiguous communication events?

## Initial minimum subset

After participant-facing intake is authorized, and before selecting a
participant-specific access route or model, answer items:

`1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 13, 18, 19, 22, 23, 30, 31`.

The remaining items can be completed during formal profile assessment.
